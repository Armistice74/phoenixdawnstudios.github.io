Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 31, 2026

This policy explains what Snooze Crush (おはよう推し in Japanese), our alarm app for iPhone, and this website do with information about you: what stays on your iPhone, what the app sends, who receives it, why, and for how long. We've written it in plain English and tried to leave nothing out.

At a glance

  • No sign-up. You never create an account with us. The app knows your purchases by a code made from an ID Apple gives your Apple Account. It isn't your name or email, but it's the same on all your iPhones, so we treat it as information about you.
  • Your alarms, plans, and settings stay on your iPhone. We can't see them.
  • The app goes online for a few clear reasons: our purchase service, RevenueCat, every time you open the app or come back to it; our download server, when it downloads a Pack Crush's videos; this website, to check Pack release dates if you asked to be notified; and emails, only when you choose to send one.
  • No ads, no tracking, and we never sell your information. We use RevenueCat's purchase dashboards to understand our sales, and that's all.
  • Apple handles payments. We never see your card or bank details.
  • For ages 16 and up. Where the law calls for it, the app checks your age range on your iPhone. The result is never stored or sent.
  • This website uses no cookies and no analytics.

3. What stays on your iPhone

To work, the app saves the following in its private storage on your iPhone:

  • Your alarms: times, repeat days, any labels you type, whether each alarm is on, and snooze settings.
  • Your Crush plans: which Crush wakes you on which day.
  • Your settings, such as the app's language, which Crush appears on Home, whether background music is on, and whether the evening reminder is on.
  • Your Pack Crushes: a copy of which Crushes you've unlocked, so your alarms still ring with them when you're offline.
  • Purchase bookkeeping: your purchase ID (see Your purchase ID) and RevenueCat's saved copy of your purchases, any purchase still waiting for approval, and which Crushes a refund took away, so the app can tell you before you buy when your next draw gives one of them back.
  • Packs you asked to hear about, and their release dates once we announce them.
  • Your last call: which Crush called, whether it was a wake-up or a snooze, when, in which language, and which lines played. It's used only to fill in a Help report if you choose to send one.
  • Small bits of app memory, such as whether you've seen the welcome screen and which clip played last, so clips can take turns.
  • Downloaded videos of your Pack Crushes, in versions of the app that download them.

Apart from your purchase ID and purchases, which RevenueCat also holds (see Purchases and RevenueCat), none of this is sent to us, and we have no way to see it. Deleting the app deletes it. Your iPhone's own backups (iCloud Backup or a computer backup) may include it, depending on your settings; Apple manages those backups, and we can't access them.

4. Purchases and RevenueCat

Snooze Crush uses RevenueCat, Inc., a company in the United States, to handle in-app purchases. The app connects to RevenueCat every time it opens, when you come back to it, and when you buy or restore something, even if you never open the Packs tab. That's how it knows which Crushes you own on every iPhone you use.

Your purchase ID

Apple gives every Apple Account an ID for each app it downloads (Apple calls it the appTransactionID). It's the same on every iPhone signed in to that Apple Account, and after you reinstall. The app turns it into a code, “ast_” followed by a SHA-256 hash of Apple's ID, and uses that code as your customer ID at RevenueCat. The code isn't your name, your email, or your Apple Account login. But because it stays the same on all your iPhones, it does single out your Apple Account, so we treat it as personal information.

RevenueCat also receives Apple's signed record of your download of the app (the “AppTransaction”), which contains Apple's original ID, so RevenueCat can confirm your purchases are genuine.

What RevenueCat receives

  • your purchase ID, described above;
  • your purchase history for Snooze Crush from the App Store: which products, prices and currency, dates, App Store transaction numbers, your App Store country, and whether a purchase was refunded;
  • technical details needed to answer the app, such as the app's version, your iOS version, your language and region settings, and your IP address (which any internet connection shares). RevenueCat's software also sends the identifier iOS gives the app for your iPhone (Apple calls it the “identifier for vendor”).

How we use it

  • to confirm purchases are genuine, give you your Crushes, and remove them if Apple refunds a purchase;
  • to give you the same Crushes on every iPhone you use with that Apple Account, and when you restore;
  • to let our download server check which Crushes you've unlocked (see Pack video downloads);
  • to understand our business through RevenueCat's purchase dashboards, for example how many people buy each product, sales by country, and refunds (see Analytics); and
  • to answer you if you write to us about a purchase.

We don't use it for advertising, we don't track you across other companies' apps or websites, we don't combine it with other companies' data, and we never sell it.

What Apple does

You make every purchase through the App Store with your Apple Account. Apple processes your payment, keeps your purchase history, and handles refunds, under Apple's Privacy Policy. Apple sends us sales and financial reports, which we use for accounting and taxes; they don't include your name or contact details. If you send us the Order ID from your App Store receipt, we can find your purchase records at RevenueCat by it, for example to help with a problem or a deletion request.

5. Pack video downloads

In versions of the app that download Pack Crushes' videos instead of including them, the app downloads a Crush's videos after you buy them. It gets them from our own download server, which runs on the network of Cloudflare, Inc., with the videos stored in Cloudflare's storage.

  • The app sends the server your purchase ID and the list of Crushes to download. The server asks RevenueCat which Crushes that purchase ID has unlocked, then gives back download links that open only those Crushes' videos and expire after 6 hours.
  • Like any server, it sees your iPhone's IP address. It uses it only to count requests, so nobody can overload the server, and forgets it within about a minute.
  • The server keeps no logs and stores nothing about you. It never receives Apple's original ID, only the hashed purchase ID.
  • The videos are saved on your iPhone (see What stays on your iPhone).

6. Pack release calendar

If you tap Notify me on a Pack that's coming soon, the app reads a small public file on this website that lists Pack release dates (phoenixdawnstudios.com/app/packs.json), so it can schedule the release alert on your iPhone. It reads it only while you're waiting for a Pack the app doesn't sell yet: when you tap Notify me, and when you open the app or come back to it, at most once every 6 hours, and for up to two weeks after a release date passes, in case the date moves. The request doesn't include your name, your purchase ID, a device ID, or which Packs you follow. It's an ordinary web request, recorded in our web host's standard server logs like any visit to this site (see This website). If you never tap Notify me, the app never makes this request.

7. Help reports and emails

In Settings › Help, Report a problem and Suggest a fix to a line, and the report button at the end of a call, open a pre-filled email to suggestions@phoenixdawnstudios.com in your mail app (or your iPhone's share sheet, if there's no mail app). Nothing is sent unless you send it, and you can read, change, or delete anything in it first. The pre-filled part contains:

  • the app's version and build number, your iOS version, and your iPhone's model;
  • the app's language;
  • in versions that download Pack videos: which Pack Crushes you've unlocked, whether each one's videos are on your iPhone (and any download error), and how much free storage your iPhone has;
  • your last call: which Crush, wake-up or snooze, the date and time, the language, and which lines played, in order; and
  • if you flagged a line during a call: that line and when you flagged it.

It doesn't include your purchase ID, your alarms, or your Apple Account. When you send it, or any other email to us, we also receive your email address, the name your email shows, and whatever you write or attach.

Email to our addresses is forwarded by our domain registrar, Porkbun, to a mailbox we keep with Google (Gmail). We use emails only to answer you, fix problems, improve lines, and keep ordinary business records. We won't add you to a mailing list unless you ask. Please don't send sensitive information, such as payment card numbers, by email.

8. Alarms and notifications

Every alarm and notification is scheduled on your iPhone. The app doesn't use push notifications from a server, so no push token or device address is ever created or sent.

  • Alarms ring through the iPhone's built-in alarm system (AlarmKit) when you allow it, on iOS 26.2 and later. Otherwise they arrive as Time Sensitive notifications.
  • Tomorrow's Crush reminder, at 8 PM if tomorrow's alarm has no Crush yet. Turn it off in Settings › Notifications.
  • A release alert, once, for a Pack you tapped Notify me on.

Alarm labels you type and your Crush's name can appear on your Lock Screen.

9. The age check

Snooze Crush is for ages 16 and up. Where the law calls for it, the app asks your iPhone which age range you're in, using Apple's Declared Age Range feature. Apple decides what to share, based on your Apple Account and the choices you or your parent make, and the app learns only a range, never your birthday.

  • If the range shows you're under 16, the app stops at a notice that it's for ages 16 and up, and doesn't connect to RevenueCat or our download server.
  • If it shows you're 16 or 17, the app offers only purchases with fixed contents, not draws.

The check runs on your iPhone. The age range is kept only in the app's memory while it's open. It's never saved, and never sent to us or anyone else.

The app asks only for what an alarm clock needs. You can change these at any time in your iPhone's Settings. If you turn one off, your alarms may not ring or appear the way they should.

11. Analytics and what Apple tells us

The app has no analytics, advertising, or crash-reporting tools of its own. We do use RevenueCat's purchase dashboards for business analytics: they're built from the purchase records described in Purchases and RevenueCat and show us things like sales, refunds, and how many customers buy each product. That's why Apple's privacy label for Snooze Crush lists your purchase ID and purchase history as used for “Analytics” as well as for the app's functions.

Apple also gives us App Store statistics, such as downloads and sales. If you've chosen in your iPhone's settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may also give us usage statistics and crash reports. Apple de-identifies these, and we use them only to find and fix problems. You can turn this sharing off in Settings › Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements.

12. This website

phoenixdawnstudios.com is a simple, static website.

  • No cookies, no tracking. We don't set cookies or use tracking technologies such as tracking pixels or browser fingerprinting. The site follows your device's light or dark setting automatically and stores nothing in your browser. Its only script plays the Crushes' videos when you scroll to them.

  • Server logs. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, run by GitHub, Inc. Like nearly every web host, GitHub automatically records standard technical information when you visit, including your IP address, for security, under the GitHub General Privacy Statement. We don't receive these logs.
  • Links. This site links to other companies' websites, such as Apple. Their own privacy policies apply when you visit them.

15. Deleting your information

  • On your iPhone: delete alarms in the app at any time, or delete the app to remove everything it stores. (“Offload App” keeps your data, so choose “Delete App” to remove it.)
  • Your RevenueCat record: email hello@phoenixdawnstudios.com with the Order ID from one of your Snooze Crush receipts (in Apple's receipt email, or at reportaproblem.apple.com). We'll find your record by it and delete it within 30 days. If you've never bought anything, there's no Order ID to find it by, and the record holds only your purchase ID and technical details, with nothing that names you.

Please note: deleting your RevenueCat record doesn't cancel or refund purchases, which stay with your Apple Account. And the next time Snooze Crush opens on an iPhone signed in to that Apple Account, it creates the record again and brings back your purchases from Apple, because that's how it gives you your Crushes. To keep the record from coming back, delete the app from your iPhones before you ask us.

Your purchase history kept by Apple is Apple's to manage. Contact Apple about it.

23. Security

The best protection is not having the data in the first place. The app keeps your alarms and settings on your iPhone, and we keep very little ourselves. For what we do hold, we use reasonable safeguards: every connection the app makes is encrypted (HTTPS); our download server receives only the hashed purchase ID, keeps no logs, and hands out links that expire and open only the Crushes you've unlocked; and we limit access to the people who need it and choose providers with strong security practices. Your iPhone's passcode and software updates protect what the app stores. No way of storing or sending information is completely secure, so we can't promise absolute security.
