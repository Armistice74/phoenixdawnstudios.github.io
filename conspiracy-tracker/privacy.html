Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 8, 2026

This policy explains what Conspiracy Tracker, our evidence-board app for iPhone and iPad, does with information about you: what stays on your device, what the app sends, who receives it, why, and for how long. We've written it in plain English and tried to leave nothing out.

At a glance

  • No sign-up. You never create an account with us.
  • Your cases stay on your device. Everything you pin to a board, from photos and files to notes, links, recordings and strings, is saved on your iPhone or iPad. We can't see it, and it's never sent to us.
  • The app goes online for a few clear reasons: our purchase service, RevenueCat; the websites you add as links, to show their preview, and the pages you open; a free PDF reader's code, from a public code library, when you add a PDF; and emails, only when you choose to send one.
  • No ads, no tracking, and we never sell your information. We use RevenueCat's purchase dashboards to understand our sales, and that's all.
  • Apple handles payments. We never see your card or bank details.
  • For ages 13 and up. Where the law calls for it, the app checks your age range on your device. The result is never stored or sent.
  • Our website uses no cookies and no analytics (see This website).

1. Who we are

“Phoenix Dawn Studios,” “we,” “us,” and “our” mean Phoenix Dawn Studios LLC, a Michigan limited liability company in the United States. We make Conspiracy Tracker. We decide how the information described here is used, so we're its “controller” under laws like the GDPR.

This policy covers Conspiracy Tracker and emails you send us about it. Our website, phoenixdawnstudios.com, is covered in This website. This policy doesn't cover services run by other companies, such as Apple's App Store, iCloud, your mail app, or the websites you save in the app. Those have their own privacy policies.

Questions about privacy? Contact us using the details in Contact us.

3. What stays on your device

To work, the app saves the following in its private storage on your iPhone or iPad:

  • Your cases: their titles, boards, folders, cards, notes, theories, dates, highlights, and strings, and everything you've typed.
  • Copies of what you pin up: photos, PDFs and other files, video and audio clips, and the preview pictures of web links. The app keeps its own copy, so a card still works if the original is moved or deleted.
  • Your library: your layers, string colors and names, and any card formats you've made.
  • Your settings, such as sound, haptics, the board style, and which tips you've seen.
  • Which cases are locked, and whether you've unlocked unlimited cases.

None of this is sent to us, and we have no way to see it. What you put on your boards may include information about other people, such as the people in a case you're following. That stays on your device too, and we never receive it.

Deleting the app deletes it. Your device's own backups (iCloud Backup or a computer backup) may include it, depending on your settings; Apple manages those backups, and we can't access them.

Files you make. A backup file, a printed or saved case file (PDF), and an evidence photo are made on your device. The app opens your device's share sheet, and you choose where each one goes, for example the Files app, AirDrop, or a message. They don't go through us. A backup file isn't locked: anyone who has it can open every case in it, including locked ones, so keep it somewhere private.

4. Purchases and RevenueCat

Conspiracy Tracker is free to download, and a one-time purchase unlocks unlimited cases. The app uses RevenueCat, Inc., a company in the United States, to handle that purchase. The app connects to RevenueCat when it opens, when you come back to it, and when you buy or restore, even if you never buy anything. That's how it knows whether your Apple Account has unlocked the app, on every device you use.

What RevenueCat receives

  • a customer ID for the app. It isn't your name, your email, or your Apple Account login. But it's linked to your purchase records, so we treat it as personal information;
  • your purchase history for Conspiracy Tracker from the App Store: which product, the price and currency, dates, App Store transaction numbers, your App Store country, and whether a purchase was refunded; and
  • technical details needed to answer the app, such as the app's version, your iOS or iPadOS version, your language and region settings, and your IP address (which any internet connection shares). RevenueCat's software also sends the identifier iOS gives the app for your device (Apple calls it the “identifier for vendor”). RevenueCat uses your IP address to work out which country you're in, then keeps only the country, which can show up in our sales-by-country dashboards.

RevenueCat never receives your cases or anything you've pinned to them.

How we use it

  • to confirm your purchase is genuine, unlock unlimited cases, and return the app to its free limits if Apple refunds the purchase;
  • to give you the unlock on every device you use with that Apple Account, and when you restore;
  • to understand our business through RevenueCat's purchase dashboards, for example how many people buy and from which countries (see Analytics); and
  • to answer you if you write to us about a purchase.

We don't use it for advertising, we don't track you across other companies' apps or websites, we don't combine it with other companies' data, and we never sell it.

What Apple does

You make the purchase through the App Store with your Apple Account. Apple processes your payment, keeps your purchase history, and handles refunds, under Apple's Privacy Policy. Apple sends us sales and financial reports, which we use for accounting and taxes; they don't include your name or contact details. If you send us the Order ID from your App Store receipt, we can find your purchase records at RevenueCat by it, for example to help with a problem or a deletion request.

5. Web links

  • Adding a link. When you add a web link to a board, your device asks that website for the page, once, to read its title, short description, and preview picture, and then downloads the picture. The request goes straight from your device to that website, not through us. Like any visit, the website sees your IP address and that the page was requested. If you'd rather a website not get that request, write the address on a note instead.
  • Opening a link. Tapping a link opens the page in a browser view inside the app, which Apple provides. What you do there is between you and that website, under its own privacy policy. The app can't see what you browse.

6. Reading PDFs

When you add a PDF, the app reads its pages on your device, to show them on the card and to let you search and highlight their words. To do that, it uses pdf.js, a free, open-source PDF reader. The app loads the reader's code from a public code library, cdnjs (run by Cloudflare, Inc.), or jsDelivr if cdnjs doesn't answer. It also loads fonts and character maps from jsDelivr when a PDF needs them. These are ordinary web requests: the library sees your IP address and which version of the reader was requested, like any website.

Your PDF itself never leaves your device. It's handed to the reader on your device, and nothing about it is sent anywhere.

7. Suggestions and emails

In the app's Help, Send a suggestion opens a new, empty email to suggestions@phoenixdawnstudios.com in your mail app. Nothing is sent unless you send it, and the app adds nothing to it: no case, no device details, no ID. When you send it, or any other email to us, we receive your email address, the name your email shows, and whatever you write or attach. Please don't attach anything from a case you'd rather keep private.

Email to our addresses is forwarded by our domain registrar, Porkbun, to a mailbox we keep with Google (Gmail). We use emails only to answer you, fix problems, improve the app, and keep ordinary business records. We won't add you to a mailing list unless you ask. Please don't send sensitive information, such as payment card numbers, by email.

8. Face ID and case locks

When you lock a case, your device's own Face ID (or its passcode) opens it. Face ID is handled entirely by your device: the app never sees your face, your face data, or your passcode. It only learns whether you were recognized. Locked cases are stored on your device like any other case; the lock keeps them closed inside the app.

9. Features that run on your device

Some versions of the app include these features. Each one runs on your device with Apple's built-in tools, and none of them sends your cases to us or to any AI service:

  • Scanning paper and reading words in photos, so you can search and highlight them.
  • Reading speech in your video and audio clips into words, so you can search them.
  • Sharing into the app from other apps, such as a photo or a web page from Safari.
  • iCloud backup and sync, if you turn it on. Your cases are then also stored in your own iCloud account, by Apple, under Apple's Privacy Policy, so they can appear on your other devices. We can't see what's in your iCloud account.

10. The age check

Conspiracy Tracker is for ages 13 and up. Where the law calls for it, the app asks your device which age range you're in, using Apple's Declared Age Range feature. Apple decides what to share, based on your Apple Account and the choices you or your parent make, and the app learns only a range, never your birthday.

If the range shows you're under 13, the app stops at a notice that it's for ages 13 and up, and doesn't connect to RevenueCat. The check runs on your device. The age range is kept only in the app's memory while it's open. It's never saved, and never sent to us or anyone else.

11. Permissions the app asks for

The app asks only when you first use the feature that needs it, and you can change each one at any time in your device's Settings. If you turn one off, only that feature stops working.

12. Analytics and what Apple tells us

The app has no analytics, advertising, or crash-reporting tools of its own. We do use RevenueCat's purchase dashboards for business analytics: they're built from the purchase records described in Purchases and RevenueCat and show us things like sales, refunds, and how many customers buy. That's why Apple's privacy label for Conspiracy Tracker lists your customer ID (“User ID”), your purchase history, the identifier iOS gives the app for your device (“Device ID”) and the country RevenueCat works out from your IP address (“Coarse Location”) as used for “Analytics” as well as for the app's functions.

Apple also gives us App Store statistics, such as downloads and sales. If you've chosen in your device's settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may also give us usage statistics and crash reports. Apple de-identifies these, and we use them only to find and fix problems. You can turn this sharing off in Settings › Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements.

14. How long we keep information

  • On your device: until you delete it in the app or delete the app. Copies may remain in your own device backups and backup files until those are replaced or deleted.
  • Your RevenueCat record (customer ID, purchase history, technical details): for as long as it may be needed to give you back your purchase. The app can't tell us when you've stopped using it, so in practice we keep it until you ask us to delete it, or until we stop using RevenueCat. We delete it within 30 days of a verified request (see Deleting your information).
  • Suggestions and other emails: up to 3 years after our last exchange, then deleted. We delete them sooner if you ask, unless we need them for a legal claim.
  • Apple's sales and financial reports: 7 years, for tax and accounting. They don't include your name or contact details.
  • Your age range: never stored.

15. Deleting your information

  • On your device: delete a case in the app (press and hold it on the shelf), or delete the app to remove everything it stores. (“Offload App” keeps your data, so choose “Delete App” to remove it.) Delete any backup files you've saved, too.
  • Your RevenueCat record: email hello@phoenixdawnstudios.com with the Order ID from your Conspiracy Tracker receipt (in Apple's receipt email, or at reportaproblem.apple.com). We'll find your record by it and delete it within 30 days. If you've never bought anything, there's no Order ID to find it by, and the record holds only your customer ID and technical details, with nothing that names you.
  • Emails: ask us, and we'll delete your emails.

Please note: deleting your RevenueCat record doesn't cancel or refund your purchase, which stays with your Apple Account. The next time you restore your purchase, or open the app on a device signed in to that Apple Account, a new record may be created so the app can give you the unlock again. To keep it from coming back, delete the app from your devices before you ask us.

Your purchase history kept by Apple is Apple's to manage. Contact Apple about it.

16. Children and teens

Conspiracy Tracker is for ages 13 and up, as our Terms of Use say, and it isn't directed to children. We don't knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. Where the law calls for it, the app's on-device age check stops users under 13 before the app connects to RevenueCat, and the age range is never stored or sent.

If you believe a child under 13 has sent us personal information, for example by email, contact us and we'll delete it.

17. Your rights, wherever you live

Wherever you live, you can ask us:

  • what personal information we have about you, and for a copy of it;
  • to correct it or delete it; and
  • to stop using it for a particular purpose.

To make a request, email hello@phoenixdawnstudios.com. To find your purchase records, we'll need the Order ID from your receipt; we won't ask for more than we need to confirm a request is genuine. We'll respond within 10 days, and there's no charge. Where the law allows, you can use an authorized agent to make a request for you. We won't treat you differently for using your rights. If we turn down your request, you can ask us to reconsider by replying to our decision.

We hold nothing from your cases, so there's nothing of them for us to send, correct, or delete: you control them in the app. You can also withdraw a permission, or stop sharing analytics with developers, at any time in your device's Settings.

  • Categories we collect:

    • geolocation data: only your country, which RevenueCat works out from your IP address.

  • Your rights: you can ask to know, access, correct, and delete your personal information (see Your rights), and we won't discriminate against you for doing so.
  • Opt-out signals and Do Not Track: we don't sell, share, or track you across other sites and apps, so there's nothing to opt out of. The app works the same either way.

20. Security

The best protection is not having the data in the first place. Your cases stay on your device, and we keep very little ourselves. For what we do hold, we use reasonable safeguards: the app's connections to RevenueCat are encrypted (HTTPS), and we limit access to the people who need it and choose providers with strong security practices. Your device's passcode, Face ID, and software updates protect what the app stores, and a case lock adds a second door inside the app. No way of storing or sending information is completely secure, so we can't promise absolute security.

21. Changes to this policy

We'll update this policy as the app changes. When we do, we'll post the new version on this page and update the effective date at the top. If a change is significant, for example if the app would start sending new kinds of information off your device, we'll tell you clearly before it takes effect, in the app or on this website, and ask for your consent where the law requires it.
