Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 12, 2026

This policy explains what Conspiracy Tracker, our evidence-board app for iPhone and iPad, does with information about you: what stays on your device, what the app sends, who receives it, why, and for how long. We've written it in plain English and tried to leave nothing out.

At a glance

  • No sign-up. You never create an account with us.
  • Your cases stay on your device. Everything you pin to a board, from photos and files to notes, links, recordings and strings, is saved on your iPhone or iPad (and, only if you turn on iCloud backup, in your own iCloud Drive). We can't see it, and it's never sent to us.
  • The app collects no personal data, and sends nothing to us. The only personal information we get is what you choose to send us, such as an email.
  • The app goes online only for a few clear reasons, and never to us: the websites you add or share as links, to show their preview (sent without cookies); Apple's App Store, to check, buy or restore the unlock; your own iCloud Drive, only if you turn on iCloud backup; and Apple, to download its speech model for your language the first time the app writes out a clip. A link you tap opens in your browser, never inside the app.
  • No ads, no tracking, and we never sell your information. The app has no analytics or crash-reporting tools.
  • Apple handles payments. We never see your card or bank details, and the app tells us nothing about your purchase.
  • No minimum age. Only where Apple tells the app the law requires it, the app asks for your age range on your device. It doesn't stop anyone from using the app, and the result is never stored or sent.
  • Our website uses no cookies and no analytics (see This website).

This policy doesn't cover services run by other companies, such as Apple's App Store, iCloud, your mail app, or the websites you save in the app. Those have their own privacy policies.

3. What stays on your device

To work, the app saves the following in its private storage on your iPhone or iPad:

  • Your cases: their titles, boards, folders, cards, notes, theories, dates, highlights, and strings, and everything you've typed.
  • Copies of what you pin up or share into the app: photos, scanned pages, PDFs and other files, video and audio clips, and the preview pictures of web links. The app keeps its own copy, so a card still works if the original is moved or deleted.
  • What the app reads on your device: the words on your scans, photos and PDF pages, and the transcripts of your video and audio clips (see Features that run on your device).
  • Your library: your layers, string colors and names, and any card formats you've made.
  • Your settings, such as sound, haptics, the board style, and which tips you've seen.
  • Which cases are locked, and whether you've unlocked unlimited cases.

None of this is sent to us, and we have no way to see it. What you put on your boards may include information about other people, such as the people in a case you're following. That stays on your device too, and we never receive it.

Deleting the app deletes it. Your device's own backups (iCloud Backup or a computer backup) may include it, depending on your settings; Apple manages those backups, and we can't access them. If you turned on the app's own iCloud backup, its copies stay in your iCloud Drive until you delete them.

Files you make. A backup file, a printed or saved case file (PDF), and an evidence photo are made on your device. The app opens your device's share sheet, and you choose where each one goes, for example the Files app, AirDrop, or a message. They don't go through us. A backup file isn't locked: anyone who has it can open every case in it, including locked ones, so keep it somewhere private.

4. Purchases

Conspiracy Tracker is free to download, and free to use with one case at a time, with one case folder in it. A one-time purchase through the App Store unlocks unlimited cases and folders.

You pay through Apple's App Store. The app uses Apple's StoreKit, on your device, to ask the App Store whether your Apple Account has bought the unlock, and to restore it on your other devices. The app sends nothing about your purchase to us, or to any company other than Apple, and it doesn't use any other purchase service. If Apple refunds the unlock, the app goes back to the free limits, and your cases and folders stay.

What Apple does

You make the purchase through the App Store with your Apple Account. Apple processes your payment, keeps your purchase history, and handles refunds and Ask to Buy, under Apple's Privacy Policy. Apple sends us sales and financial reports, which we use for accounting and taxes; they don't include your name or contact details. If you write to us about a purchase, we know only what you tell us.

5. Web links

  • Adding a link. When you add a web link to a board, or share one into the app from another app, your device asks that website for the page, once, to read its title, short description, and preview picture, and then downloads the picture from wherever the page says it's kept (sometimes another website). The requests go straight from your device to those websites, not through us. Like any visit, each website sees your IP address, what was requested, and that the request came from Conspiracy Tracker. The page and its picture are fetched without cookies: the app sends none with these requests. The picture is saved on your device, with the card. If you'd rather a website not get that request, write the address on a note instead.
  • Opening a link. Tapping Open on a link card opens the page in your browser (Safari, unless you've chosen another), outside Conspiracy Tracker (only web addresses open). The app never opens web pages inside itself. What you do there is between you, your browser, and that website, under their own privacy policies. The app can't see what you browse.

6. Reading PDFs

PDFs are read on your device, with no internet needed. The app reads a PDF's pages with pdf.js, a free, open-source PDF reader that's built into the app, together with the fonts and character maps it needs. Nothing is downloaded to read a PDF, and your PDF never leaves your device.

7. Suggestions and emails

In the app's Help, Send a suggestion opens a new, empty email to suggestions@phoenixdawnstudios.com in your mail app. Nothing is sent unless you send it, and the app adds nothing to it: no case, no device details, no ID. When you send it, or any other email to us, we receive your email address, the name your email shows, and whatever you write or attach. Please don't attach anything from a case you'd rather keep private.

Email to our addresses is forwarded by our domain registrar, Porkbun, to a mailbox we keep with Google (Gmail). Calls, texts and voicemails to our phone line come through Google Voice, which shows us your phone number. We use emails and other messages only to answer you, fix problems, improve the app, and keep ordinary business records. We won't add you to a mailing list unless you ask. Please don't send sensitive information, such as payment card numbers, by email.

8. Face ID and case locks

When you lock a case, your device's own Face ID or Touch ID (or its passcode) opens it. Face ID and Touch ID are handled entirely by your device: the app never sees your face, your fingerprint, their data, or your passcode. It only learns whether you were recognized. Locked cases are stored on your device like any other case; the lock keeps them closed inside the app only. Backups have locked cases in them, in readable form: a backup file you save, and the copies iCloud backup makes if you turn it on. Anyone who can open a backup can read every case in it.

9. Features that run on your device

These features run on your device with Apple's built-in tools. None of them sends your cases to us or to any AI service.

Scanning paper and reading words

Scan paper uses Apple's document camera to photograph your pages. The app then reads the words on your scans, on the photos you pin up, and on PDF pages that are only pictures, with Apple's text recognition, so you can search them, highlight them and tie strings to them. All of this happens on your device: the pictures and their words never leave it.

Transcripts of your video and audio clips

On an iPhone or iPad with iOS or iPadOS 26 or later, the app writes out what's said in your video and audio clips with Apple's on-device speech recognition, so you can read and search it. It reads the clips you've added; it never uses your microphone, and the audio never leaves your device. It listens for your device's main language. The first time, your device may download Apple's speech model for that language from Apple; your clips aren't part of that download. On earlier versions of iOS and iPadOS, transcripts aren't available.

Sharing into the app

When you share a photo, file, link or text to Conspiracy Tracker from another app, the app's share extension copies it into storage on your device that only the app and its extension can use (an App Group), and the app then moves it into its Inbox. What you share stays on your device and is never sent to us. A shared link's preview is fetched as described in Web links.

iCloud backup

  • Off until you turn it on, in the app's Settings (Back up to iCloud Drive).
  • What it does: after you change something, the app saves a backup file of your cases, the same kind Save a backup file makes, in your own iCloud Drive, in the app's folder that you can see in the Files app. It does this at most once a day, usually when you leave the app, or straight away when you tap Back up to iCloud now.
  • Locked cases are included, and readable. Anyone who can open your iCloud Drive can read every case in a backup, locked ones too. If a case is locked when you turn it on, the app asks for Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode first.
  • How many: it keeps the two newest copies from each of your devices, plus one at least a week old and one at least a month old once there are copies that old (with the copies on their way to those ages, six at most per device), and deletes that device's other older ones.
  • Restoring: on another iPhone or iPad signed in to the same Apple Account, Restore from iCloud brings a copy back. Like any restore, it replaces the cases on that device.
  • Deleting: turning it off stops new copies. The copies already there stay until you delete them in the Files app.
  • Who holds it: Apple stores your iCloud Drive under Apple's Privacy Policy and uses your own iCloud storage for it. We can't see your backups. Under standard data protection, Apple holds the encryption keys for iCloud Drive; it's end-to-end encrypted only if you turn on Advanced Data Protection (see Apple's iCloud data security overview).

10. The age check

Conspiracy Tracker has no minimum age, and the age check never stops anyone from using it. Some laws (today, in some U.S. states) ask apps to check users' ages through the App Store. Only where Apple tells the app that the law requires it, the app asks which age range you're in, using Apple's Declared Age Range feature. Everywhere else, it never asks. Apple decides what to share, based on your Apple Account and the choices you or your parent make, and the app learns only a range, never your birthday.

The app uses the answer only to follow those laws. It never blocks or limits anything because of it, and if you choose not to share your age range, or anything goes wrong, it carries on as normal. The one thing it changes: if you share that you're under 13, Send a suggestion in Help asks a parent or guardian to email us for you, instead of opening an email. The check runs on your device. The age range is kept only in the app's memory while it's open. It's never saved, and never sent to us or anyone else.

Where the law calls for it, Apple asks a parent or guardian to approve a minor's downloads and in-app purchases, and we rely on that approval.

12. Analytics and what Apple tells us

The app has no analytics, advertising, or crash-reporting tools of its own, and no purchase service (see Purchases). It sends us nothing about you or how you use it.

Apple also gives us App Store statistics, such as downloads and sales. If you've chosen in your device's settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may also give us usage statistics and crash reports. Apple de-identifies these, and we use them only to find and fix problems. You can turn this sharing off in Settings › Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements.

16. Children and teens

Conspiracy Tracker is a general-audience app, made for people who follow true crime and do their own research. It has no minimum age, as our Terms of Use say, but it isn't directed to children.

Apart from an email you choose to send us, the app doesn't collect personal information from anyone, including children. There's no account, no ads, and no analytics, your cases never reach us, and you pay for a purchase through Apple's App Store. Only where Apple tells the app the law requires it, the app's on-device age check asks Apple for your age range, but it doesn't stop anyone from using the app, and the age range is never stored or sent. Where the law calls for it, Apple asks a parent or guardian to approve a minor's downloads and purchases.

If you're under 13, please ask a parent or guardian before you email us. If we learn an email came from a child under 13, we'll use it only to answer that one message, we won't share it, and we'll delete it promptly after replying. Parents and guardians can ask us to delete a child's email at any time, and we will.

17. Your rights, wherever you live

Wherever you live, you can ask us:

  • what personal information we have about you, and for a copy of it;
  • to correct it or delete it; and
  • to stop using it for a particular purpose.

To make a request, email hello@phoenixdawnstudios.com. We won't ask for more than we need to confirm a request is genuine. We'll respond within 10 days, and there's no charge. Where the law allows, you can use an authorized agent to make a request for you. We won't treat you differently for using your rights. If we turn down your request, you can ask us to reconsider by replying to our decision.

We hold nothing from your cases or your purchase, so there's nothing of them for us to send, correct, or delete: you control your cases in the app, and Apple keeps your purchase history. You can also withdraw a permission, or stop sharing analytics with developers, at any time in your device's Settings.
