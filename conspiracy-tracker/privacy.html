Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 8, 2026

This policy explains what Conspiracy Tracker, our evidence-board app for iPhone and iPad, does with information about you: what stays on your device, what the app sends, who receives it, why, and for how long. We've written it in plain English and tried to leave nothing out.

At a glance

  • No sign-up. You never create an account with us.
  • Your cases stay on your device. Everything you pin to a board, from photos and files to notes, links, recordings and strings, is saved on your iPhone or iPad. We can't see it, and it's never sent to us.
  • The app collects no personal data, and sends nothing to us. The only personal information we get is what you choose to send us, such as an email.
  • The app goes online only for a few clear reasons, and never to us: the websites you add as links, to show their preview; a free PDF reader's code, from a public code library, when you add a PDF; Apple's App Store, to check, buy or restore the unlock; and your own iCloud, if you turn it on in a version that has it. A link you tap opens in the Safari app.
  • No ads, no tracking, and we never sell your information. The app has no analytics or crash-reporting tools.
  • Apple handles payments. We never see your card or bank details, and the app tells us nothing about your purchase.
  • No minimum age. Where the law calls for it, the app checks your age range on your device. It doesn't stop anyone from using the app, and the result is never stored or sent.
  • Our website uses no cookies and no analytics (see This website).

1. Who we are

“Phoenix Dawn Studios,” “we,” “us,” and “our” mean Phoenix Dawn Studios LLC, a Michigan limited liability company in the United States. We make Conspiracy Tracker. We decide how the information described here is used, so we're its “controller” under laws like the GDPR.

This policy covers Conspiracy Tracker and the emails and other messages you send us about it. Our website, phoenixdawnstudios.com, is covered in This website. This policy doesn't cover services run by other companies, such as Apple's App Store, iCloud, your mail app, or the websites you save in the app. Those have their own privacy policies.

Questions about privacy? Contact us using the details in Contact us.

3. What stays on your device

To work, the app saves the following in its private storage on your iPhone or iPad:

  • Your cases: their titles, boards, folders, cards, notes, theories, dates, highlights, and strings, and everything you've typed.
  • Copies of what you pin up: photos, PDFs and other files, video and audio clips, and the preview pictures of web links. The app keeps its own copy, so a card still works if the original is moved or deleted.
  • Your library: your layers, string colors and names, and any card formats you've made.
  • Your settings, such as sound, haptics, the board style, and which tips you've seen.
  • Which cases are locked, and whether you've unlocked unlimited cases.

None of this is sent to us, and we have no way to see it. What you put on your boards may include information about other people, such as the people in a case you're following. That stays on your device too, and we never receive it.

Deleting the app deletes it. Your device's own backups (iCloud Backup or a computer backup) may include it, depending on your settings; Apple manages those backups, and we can't access them.

Files you make. A backup file, a printed or saved case file (PDF), and an evidence photo are made on your device. The app opens your device's share sheet, and you choose where each one goes, for example the Files app, AirDrop, or a message. They don't go through us. A backup file isn't locked: anyone who has it can open every case in it, including locked ones, so keep it somewhere private.

4. Purchases

Conspiracy Tracker is free to download, and free to use with one case at a time, with one case folder in it. A one-time purchase through the App Store unlocks unlimited cases and folders.

The purchase is between you and Apple. The app uses Apple's StoreKit, on your device, to ask the App Store whether your Apple Account has bought the unlock, and to restore it on your other devices. The app sends nothing about your purchase to us, or to any company other than Apple, and it doesn't use any other purchase service. If Apple refunds the unlock, the app goes back to the free limits, and your cases and folders stay.

What Apple does

You make the purchase through the App Store with your Apple Account. Apple processes your payment, keeps your purchase history, and handles refunds and Ask to Buy, under Apple's Privacy Policy. Apple sends us sales and financial reports, which we use for accounting and taxes; they don't include your name or contact details. If you write to us about a purchase, we know only what you tell us.

5. Web links

  • Adding a link. When you add a web link to a board, your device asks that website for the page, once, to read its title, short description, and preview picture, and then downloads the picture from wherever the page says it's kept (sometimes another website). The requests go straight from your device to those websites, not through us. Like any visit, each website sees your IP address, what was requested, and that the request came from Conspiracy Tracker. A website can also leave a cookie in the app, as it would in a browser, and see it again the next time you add one of its pages. The picture is saved on your device, with the card. If you'd rather a website not get that request, write the address on a note instead.
  • Opening a link. Tapping Open on a link card opens the page in the Safari app, outside Conspiracy Tracker (only web addresses open). What you do there is between you, Safari, and that website, under their own privacy policies. The app can't see what you browse.

6. Reading PDFs

When you add a PDF, the app reads its pages on your device, to show them on the card and to let you search and highlight their words. To do that, it uses pdf.js, a free, open-source PDF reader. The app loads the reader's code from a public code library, cdnjs (run by Cloudflare, Inc.), or jsDelivr if cdnjs doesn't answer. It also loads fonts and character maps from jsDelivr when a PDF needs them. These are ordinary web requests: the library sees your IP address and which version of the reader was requested, like any website. They're used only to deliver the reader to your device. The app adds no ID to them and none of your PDF's content. At most, the font and character-map files a PDF needs show the library which kind of lettering it uses, such as Japanese. What the libraries do with their own logs is up to them, under Cloudflare's and jsDelivr's own policies.

Your PDF itself never leaves your device. It's handed to the reader on your device, and nothing about it is sent anywhere.

7. Suggestions and emails

In the app's Help, Send a suggestion opens a new, empty email to suggestions@phoenixdawnstudios.com in your mail app. Nothing is sent unless you send it, and the app adds nothing to it: no case, no device details, no ID. When you send it, or any other email to us, we receive your email address, the name your email shows, and whatever you write or attach. Please don't attach anything from a case you'd rather keep private.

Email to our addresses is forwarded by our domain registrar, Porkbun, to a mailbox we keep with Google (Gmail). Calls, texts and voicemails to our phone line come through Google Voice, which shows us your phone number. We use emails and other messages only to answer you, fix problems, improve the app, and keep ordinary business records. We won't add you to a mailing list unless you ask. Please don't send sensitive information, such as payment card numbers, by email.

8. Face ID and case locks

When you lock a case, your device's own Face ID or Touch ID (or its passcode) opens it. Face ID and Touch ID are handled entirely by your device: the app never sees your face, your fingerprint, their data, or your passcode. It only learns whether you were recognized. Locked cases are stored on your device like any other case; the lock keeps them closed inside the app.

9. Features that run on your device

Some versions of the app include these features. Each one runs on your device with Apple's built-in tools, and none of them sends your cases to us or to any AI service:

  • Scanning paper and reading words in photos, so you can search and highlight them.
  • Reading speech in your video and audio clips into words, so you can search them.
  • Sharing into the app from other apps, such as a photo or a web page from Safari.
  • iCloud backup and sync, if you turn it on. Your cases are then also stored in your own iCloud account, by Apple, under Apple's Privacy Policy, so they can appear on your other devices. We can't see what's in your iCloud account.

10. The age check

Conspiracy Tracker has no minimum age, and the age check never stops anyone from using it. Some laws (today, in some U.S. states) ask apps to check users' ages through the App Store. Where the law calls for it, the app asks your device which age range you're in, using Apple's Declared Age Range feature. Apple decides what to share, based on your Apple Account and the choices you or your parent make, and the app learns only a range, never your birthday.

The app uses the answer only to follow those laws. It works the same for every age, and if you choose not to share your age range, it carries on as normal. The check runs on your device. The age range is kept only in the app's memory while it's open. It's never saved, and never sent to us or anyone else.

Where the law calls for it, Apple asks a parent or guardian to approve a minor's downloads and in-app purchases, and we rely on that approval.

11. Permissions the app asks for

The app asks only when you first use the feature that needs it, and you can change each one at any time in your device's Settings. If you turn one off, only that feature stops working.

12. Analytics and what Apple tells us

The app has no analytics, advertising, or crash-reporting tools of its own, and no purchase service (see Purchases). It sends us nothing about you or how you use it.

Apple also gives us App Store statistics, such as downloads and sales. If you've chosen in your device's settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may also give us usage statistics and crash reports. Apple de-identifies these, and we use them only to find and fix problems. You can turn this sharing off in Settings › Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements.

14. How long we keep information

  • On your device: until you delete it in the app or delete the app. Copies may remain in your own device backups and backup files until those are replaced or deleted.
  • Suggestions, emails and other messages: up to 3 years after our last exchange, then deleted. We delete them sooner if you ask, unless we need them for a legal claim. If we learn an email came from a child under 13, we delete it promptly after we've replied (see Children and teens).
  • Apple's sales and financial reports: 7 years, for tax and accounting. They don't include your name or contact details.
  • Your age range: never stored.

15. Deleting your information

  • On your device: delete a case in the app (press and hold it on the shelf), or delete the app to remove everything it stores. (“Offload App” keeps your data, so choose “Delete App” to remove it.) Delete any backup files you've saved, too.
  • Emails and other messages: email hello@phoenixdawnstudios.com, and we'll delete them.

That's everything: the app sends us nothing, so we hold no other record of you to delete.

Your purchase history kept by Apple is Apple's to manage. Contact Apple about it.

16. Children and teens

Conspiracy Tracker is a general-audience app, made for people who follow true crime and do their own research. It has no minimum age, as our Terms of Use say, but it isn't directed to children.

Apart from an email you choose to send us, the app doesn't collect personal information from anyone, including children. There's no account, no ads, and no analytics, your cases never reach us, and a purchase is between you and Apple. Where the law calls for it, the app's on-device age check asks Apple for your age range, but it doesn't stop anyone from using the app, and the age range is never stored or sent. Where the law calls for it, Apple asks a parent or guardian to approve a minor's downloads and purchases.

If you're under 13, please ask a parent or guardian before you email us. If we learn an email came from a child under 13, we'll use it only to answer that one message, we won't share it, and we'll delete it promptly after replying. Parents and guardians can ask us to delete a child's email at any time, and we will.

17. Your rights, wherever you live

Wherever you live, you can ask us:

  • what personal information we have about you, and for a copy of it;
  • to correct it or delete it; and
  • to stop using it for a particular purpose.

To make a request, email hello@phoenixdawnstudios.com. We won't ask for more than we need to confirm a request is genuine. We'll respond within 10 days, and there's no charge. Where the law allows, you can use an authorized agent to make a request for you. We won't treat you differently for using your rights. If we turn down your request, you can ask us to reconsider by replying to our decision.

We hold nothing from your cases or your purchase, so there's nothing of them for us to send, correct, or delete: you control your cases in the app, and Apple keeps your purchase history. You can also withdraw a permission, or stop sharing analytics with developers, at any time in your device's Settings.

18. EEA, UK, and Switzerland

If you're in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, or Switzerland, the following also applies.

  • Controller: Phoenix Dawn Studios LLC (see Contact us).
  • What we process: the emails and other messages you choose to send us. Nothing from the app reaches us. The app's requests for the PDF reader's code go straight from your device to the code library (see Reading PDFs).
  • Legal bases:
    • our legitimate interests: answering your messages, fixing problems, keeping business records, and making the PDF reader work by loading its code from a public code library when you add a PDF (see Reading PDFs). We've weighed these interests against your rights, and you can object to them; and
    • legal obligations, such as tax and accounting rules.
  • The age check runs only on your device, under the laws that call for it, and its result never reaches us.
  • Is it required? No. The app sends us nothing, and contacting us is always your choice.
  • International transfers: we're in the United States, and so are our service providers. The safeguards for each one are listed in Who we share information with.
  • Your rights: you can ask to access, correct, delete, or restrict our use of your personal information, to receive it in a portable format, and to object to uses based on our legitimate interests. You also have the right to complain to your data protection authority (in Switzerland, the FDPIC), though we'd appreciate the chance to sort things out with you first.
  • No automated decisions: we don't make decisions with legal or similarly significant effects about you based solely on automated processing.

  • Categories we collect: identifiers, only if you contact us: your email address or phone number, the name your email shows, and your postal address if you write to us by post, along with whatever you choose to write, say or attach. The app itself collects nothing.
  • Sources: you, when you contact us.
  • Purposes: answering your messages, fixing problems, security, and legal compliance.
  • Disclosed to: the service providers in Who we share information with, for these business purposes only.

  • Sale and sharing: we don't sell personal information or share it for cross-context behavioral advertising, and we haven't done so in the past 12 months. That includes information about consumers under 16.

  • Opt-out signals and Do Not Track: we don't sell, share, or track you across other companies' apps or websites, and the app has no ads or tracking tools that would let anyone else do so, so there's nothing to opt out of. The app works the same whether or not you send a Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signal.

20. Security

The best protection is not having the data in the first place. Your cases stay on your device, the app sends us nothing, and the only personal information we hold is the messages you send us. For those, we use reasonable safeguards: we limit access to the people who need it and choose providers with strong security practices. Your device's passcode, Face ID or Touch ID, and software updates protect what the app stores, and a case lock adds a second door inside the app. No way of storing or sending information is completely secure, so we can't promise absolute security.

21. Changes to this policy

We'll update this policy as the app changes. When we do, we'll post the new version on this page and update the effective date at the top. If a change is significant, for example if the app would start sending new kinds of information off your device, we'll tell you clearly before it takes effect, in the app or on this website, and ask for your consent where the law requires it. Where the law calls for it, a parent or guardian may also be asked, through Apple, to approve a significant update before a minor can use it.
