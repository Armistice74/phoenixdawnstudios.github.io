Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 15, 2026

This policy explains what Snooze Crush (おはよう推し in Japanese), our alarm app for iPhone and Android phones, and this website do with information about you: what stays on your phone, what the app sends, who receives it, why, and for how long. Where the Android app works differently from the iPhone app, the section says so under On Android. We've written it in plain English and tried to leave nothing out.

At a glance

  • No sign-up. You never create an account with us. On iPhone, the app knows your purchases by a code made from an ID Apple gives your Apple Account. On Android, it uses a random code the app makes on your phone, and Google Play keeps a scrambled copy of it on each purchase, so the same code comes back on your other Android phones. Neither is your name or email, but each follows your account's purchases from phone to phone, so we treat it as information about you.
  • Your alarms, plans, and settings stay on your phone. We can't see them.
  • The app goes online for a few clear reasons: our purchase service, RevenueCat, every time you open the app or come back to it; our download server, when it downloads a Pack Crush's videos and, on Android, each time you open the app (to check for refunds), before your first purchase, and to find your purchases on a new phone; Expo's update service, to check for app updates each time the app starts, in versions that get updates this way; this website, to check Pack release dates if you asked to be notified; and emails and reports, only when you choose to send one.
  • No ads, no tracking, and we never sell your information. We use RevenueCat's purchase dashboards to understand our sales, and that's all.
  • Apple or Google handles payments. We never see your card or bank details.
  • For ages 16 and up. The app checks your age range with Apple (on iPhone, where the law calls for it) or Google Play (on Android, each time the app opens). The result is never stored or sent. On Android in the United States, if Google Play gives no age, the app asks once for your birth month and year; they stay on your phone and are never sent.
  • This website uses no cookies and no analytics.

3. What stays on your phone

To work, the app saves the following in its private storage on your phone:

  • Your alarms: times, repeat days, any labels you type, whether each alarm is on, and snooze settings.
  • Your Crush plans: which Crush wakes you on which day.
  • Your settings, such as the app's language, which Crush appears on Home, whether background music is on, and whether the evening reminder is on.
  • Your Pack Crushes: a copy of which Crushes you've unlocked, so your alarms still ring with them when you're offline.
  • Purchase bookkeeping: your purchase ID (see Your purchase ID) and RevenueCat's saved copy of your purchases, any purchase still waiting for approval, and which Crushes a refund took away, so the app can tell you before you buy when your next draw gives one of them back.
  • Packs you asked to hear about, and their release dates once we announce them.
  • Your last call: which Crush called, whether it was a wake-up or a snooze, when, in which language, and which lines played. It's used only to fill in a Help report if you choose to send one.
  • Small bits of app memory, such as whether you've seen the welcome screen and which clip played last, so clips can take turns.
  • Downloaded videos of your Pack Crushes, in versions of the app that download them.
  • On Android, also: your collection code (see On Android: your collection code); in the United States, if the app asked, the birth month and year you gave (see The age check on Android); and the alarm system's own records: which alarms are booked, what rang and when, your alarm words and sounds, and a short technical log of what the alarm system did (for example, “alarm booked” or “notifications are off”). The log records what the alarm system did, to help find problems (the last 300 lines are kept). It stays on your phone unless you share it in an alarm report (see On Android: the alarm report).
  • In versions that get app updates: the latest app update the app downloaded, and the random install ID it uses to ask for updates (see App updates).

Apart from what sections 4 and 5 describe (your purchase ID and purchases, which RevenueCat also holds, on Android the stamp made from your collection code, and the install ID for app updates; see Purchases and RevenueCat and App updates), none of this is sent to us unless you choose to send it, and we have no way to see it. Deleting the app deletes it. Your iPhone's own backups (iCloud Backup or a computer backup) may include it, depending on your settings; Apple manages those backups, and we can't access them.

On Android, Android's backup to your Google Account may include this information, depending on your settings; Google manages that backup, and we can't access it. The app leaves your collection code, your birth month and year, and RevenueCat's files out of that backup, so a backup can't carry your code onto someone else's phone, and your birth month and year never leave your phone.

4. Purchases and RevenueCat

Snooze Crush uses RevenueCat, Inc., a company in the United States, to handle in-app purchases. The app connects to RevenueCat every time it opens, when you come back to it, and when you buy or restore something, even if you never open the Packs tab. That's how it knows which Crushes you own on every phone you use.

Your purchase ID

Apple gives every Apple Account an ID for each app it downloads (Apple calls it the appTransactionID). It's the same on every iPhone signed in to that Apple Account, and after you reinstall. The app turns it into a code, “ast_” followed by a SHA-256 hash of Apple's ID, and uses that code as your customer ID at RevenueCat. The code isn't your name, your email, or your Apple Account login. But because it stays the same on all your iPhones, it does single out your Apple Account, so we treat it as personal information.

RevenueCat also receives Apple's signed record of your download of the app (the “AppTransaction”), which contains Apple's original ID, so RevenueCat can confirm your purchases are genuine.

On Android: your collection code

Android has no ID like Apple's that's the same on every phone, so the app makes one. The first time the app opens with a Google Account that has no Snooze Crush purchases, it creates a collection code: 64 random characters, made on your phone. On Android, your purchase ID at RevenueCat is “gpa_” followed by that code. The code also fixes the order your draws come in (see our Terms of Use). If your Google Account's only Snooze Crush purchases came from a promo code or were made outside the app, the code is worked out instead from Google's record of the earliest of those purchases (its purchase token), so it's the same on every phone; it's added to our address book the same way.

  • What Google receives. When you buy in the app, RevenueCat's software gives Google Play a scrambled copy of your purchase ID with the purchase: a SHA-256 hash, 44 characters long, which we call the stamp. Google keeps it with the purchase and uses it, under Google's own policies, to help prevent fraud. It can't be turned back into the code, and Google never receives the code itself.
  • Our address book. Our download server (see Pack video downloads) keeps a list that pairs each stamp with its purchase ID, and the date the pair was added. It holds nothing else: no name, email, Google Account, phone details, IP address, or purchase details. The app adds your pair before your first purchase. On a new phone, or after you reinstall, the app reads the stamp from a purchase your Google Account owns, sends it to our server, and gets your purchase ID back. That's how the same Crushes come back with no sign-in.
  • The order number sent with a lookup. When the app looks up your stamp, it also sends the Google Play order number of the earliest purchase that carries that stamp (it starts with “GPA.”; purchases still waiting for payment, and promo codes, have none, so then it's left out). Our server uses it only if the address book doesn't have your stamp: it then asks RevenueCat once which customer holds that purchase, and adds the missing pair only if that customer's stamp matches yours. The order number is never stored or logged.
  • The refund check. About once each time you open the app, it asks our server whether your purchase ID still owns any Pack purchases on Google Play, and which ones were refunded, so it can tell a refund from a purchase made on another Google Account. The server asks RevenueCat and answers. It stores nothing from this.
  • What Google Play tells the app. On your phone, the app asks the Google Play app which Pack purchases your Google Account owns, with their stamps and order numbers, and which country your Google Play account is in. That country decides what's sold to you and, with your phone's region setting, how the age check works.

Like Apple's ID, the code and your purchase ID aren't your name, your email, or your Google Account. But they're the same on every phone with that Google Account, so they single out your account's purchases, and we treat them as personal information.

iPhone and Android are separate. Purchases on one don't unlock Crushes on the other, and we don't link your two purchase IDs.

What RevenueCat receives

  • your purchase ID, described above;
  • your purchase history for Snooze Crush from the App Store: which products, prices and currency, dates, App Store transaction numbers, your App Store country, and whether a purchase was refunded;
  • technical details needed to answer the app, such as the app's version, your iOS version, your language and region settings, and your IP address (which any internet connection shares). RevenueCat's software also sends the identifier iOS gives the app for your iPhone (Apple calls it the “identifier for vendor”). RevenueCat uses your IP address to work out which country you're in, then drops the address and keeps only the country, which can show up in our sales-by-country dashboards.
  • On Android, the same kinds of information, from Google Play instead of the App Store: your purchase ID (“gpa_” plus your collection code); your purchase history for Snooze Crush on Google Play (which products, prices and currency, dates, Google Play order numbers and purchase tokens, your Google Play country, and whether a purchase was refunded); and technical details such as the app's version, your Android version, your phone's maker and model, your language and region settings, and your IP address, which RevenueCat uses the same way. On Android, RevenueCat's software sends no device identifier.

How we use it

  • to confirm purchases are genuine, give you your Crushes, and remove them if Apple or Google refunds a purchase;
  • to give you the same Crushes on every iPhone you use with that Apple Account, or every Android phone you use with that Google Account, and when you restore;
  • to let our download server check which Crushes you've unlocked (see Pack video downloads);
  • to understand our business through RevenueCat's purchase dashboards, for example how many people buy each product, sales by country, and refunds (see Analytics); and
  • to answer you if you write to us about a purchase.

We don't use it for advertising, we don't track you across other companies' apps or websites, we don't combine it with other companies' data, and we never sell it.

What Apple does

You make every purchase through the App Store with your Apple Account. Apple processes your payment, keeps your purchase history, and handles refunds, under Apple's Privacy Policy. Apple sends us sales and financial reports, which we use for accounting and taxes; they don't include your name or contact details. If you send us the Order ID from your App Store receipt, we can find your purchase records at RevenueCat by it, for example to help with a problem or a deletion request.

What Google does

On Android, you make every purchase through Google Play with your Google Account. Google processes your payment, keeps your purchase history, and handles refunds, under Google Play's terms and the Google Privacy Policy and Google Payments Privacy Notice. Depending on your country, the seller is us, with Google acting as our agent, or Google. Google sends us order and sales reports, which we use for accounting, taxes, and refunds. If you send us the order number from your Google Play receipt (it starts with “GPA.”), we can find your purchase records by it, for example to help with a problem or a deletion request.

5. Pack video downloads and app updates

In versions of the app that download Pack Crushes' videos instead of including them, the app downloads a Crush's videos after you buy them. It gets them from our own download server, which runs on the network of Cloudflare, Inc., with the videos stored in Cloudflare's storage.

  • The app sends the server your purchase ID and the list of Crushes to download. The server asks RevenueCat which Crushes that purchase ID has unlocked, then gives back download links that open only those Crushes' videos and expire after 6 hours.
  • Like any server, it sees your phone's IP address. It uses it only to count requests, so nobody can overload the server, and forgets it within about a minute.
  • The server keeps no logs. It never receives Apple's original ID, only the hashed purchase ID. For iPhone it stores nothing about you. On Android it also receives your stamp and, when the app looks up a stamp, a Google Play order number; it keeps only the address book of stamps and purchase IDs, and never stores the order number (see On Android: your collection code).
  • The videos are saved on your phone (see What stays on your phone).

App updates

In versions of the app that get updates this way (on Android, every version; on iPhone, versions after the first), the app can download small updates to its own code, wording, and pictures without a new version from the store. The updates come from EAS Update, a service of 650 Industries, Inc. (“Expo”), a company in the United States. The app always opens with what's already on your phone. Each time it starts, it checks for an update in the background and uses a new one the next time you fully close and reopen it.

  • What Expo receives with each check: a random install ID that the app makes on your phone the first time it checks (it isn't your purchase ID and isn't linked to it); which system your phone runs (Android or iOS); which version of the app's code is installed, and which update channel it's on; the IDs of any recent updates that failed to start; and, only if an update stopped the app from starting, the first part of that error's technical message. Like any server, Expo also sees your phone's IP address.
  • What it never receives: your purchase ID, your purchases, your alarms, your age range, your birth month and year, or anything you type.
  • Why: to send your phone the update that fits the app you have, to go back to the last working version if an update fails, and to count how many installs got updates (Expo bills us by that count). We use only those totals.
  • Who Expo is to us: our service provider. It handles this for us under its terms, not for its own advertising, and says it doesn't work out your location from the request (see Who we share information with).
  • How long: Expo deletes the error message after three days. It keeps the install ID and IP address with its records of each check for as long as it needs them to deliver updates, under its own privacy policy. Deleting the app or clearing its data ends the install ID; a new install makes a new one, unless Android restores the app from your backup.

6. Pack release calendar

If you tap Notify me on a Pack that's coming soon, the app reads a small public file on this website that lists Pack release dates (phoenixdawnstudios.com/app/packs.json), so it can schedule the release alert on your phone. It reads it only while you're waiting for a Pack the app doesn't sell yet: when you tap Notify me, and when you open the app or come back to it, at most once every 6 hours, and for up to two weeks after a release date passes, in case the date moves. The request doesn't include your name, your purchase ID, a device ID, or which Packs you follow. It's an ordinary web request, recorded in our web host's standard server logs like any visit to this site (see This website). If you never tap Notify me, the app never makes this request.

7. Help reports and emails

In Settings › Help, Report a problem and Suggest a fix to a line, and the report button at the end of a call, open a pre-filled email to suggestions@phoenixdawnstudios.com in your mail app (or your phone's share sheet, if there's no mail app). Nothing is sent unless you send it, and you can read, change, or delete anything in it first. The pre-filled part contains:

  • the app's version (and on iPhone its build number), your phone's system and version (iOS or Android), and your phone's maker and model;
  • the app's language;
  • in versions that download Pack videos: which Pack Crushes you've unlocked, whether each one's videos are on your phone (and any download error), and how much free storage your phone has;
  • your last call: which Crush, wake-up or snooze, the date and time, the language, and which lines played, in order; and
  • if you flagged a line during a call: that line and when you flagged it.

It doesn't include your purchase ID, your alarms, or your Apple Account or Google Account. When you send it, or any other email to us, we also receive your email address, the name your email shows, and whatever you write or attach.

Email to our addresses is forwarded by our domain registrar, Porkbun, to a mailbox we keep with Google (Gmail). We use emails only to answer you, fix problems, improve lines, and keep ordinary business records. We won't add you to a mailing list unless you ask. Please don't send sensitive information, such as payment card numbers, by email.

On Android: the alarm report

On Android, Settings › Help › Share alarm report makes a plain-text report that helps find out why an alarm or a purchase didn't work. The app makes it only when you tap that button, and then opens your phone's share sheet. The app sends nothing itself: the report goes only where you choose (for example, an email to us), and you can read it there before you send it. It contains:

  • the app's version and build number, your Android version, your phone's maker and model, and when the report was made;
  • the alarm system's log (see What stays on your phone): times, which Crush rang, short alarm IDs the app made up, sound file names, volume steps, and which speaker or headphones played;
  • this session's purchase checks: the steps the app took to check your purchases since it last started, how many purchases Google Play listed, our servers' and RevenueCat's answers, and any error codes; and
  • whether the app's no-backup storage holds an age answer and a collection code (only whether each one is there, never what it says), when the app was installed and last updated, and any time that storage couldn't be read or saved.

It never contains your purchase ID, your collection code or stamp, a Google Play order number or purchase token, your email address, prices, your age range, or your birth month and year. Before the share sheet opens, the app also blanks out anything that looks like an email address or a long ID. If you send the report to us, we keep it like any other email (see How long we keep information).

8. Alarms and notifications

Every alarm and notification is scheduled on your phone. The app doesn't use push notifications from a server, so no push token or device address is ever created or sent.

  • Alarms ring through the iPhone's built-in alarm system (AlarmKit) when you allow it, on iOS 26.2 and later. Otherwise they arrive as Time Sensitive notifications.
  • Tomorrow's Crush reminder, at 8 PM if tomorrow's alarm has no Crush yet. Turn it off in Settings › Notifications.
  • A release alert, once, for a Pack you tapped Notify me on.

On Android, alarms are booked with Android's alarm clock service. When one goes off, the app plays your Crush's voice at alarm volume, with a notification that has Stop and Snooze buttons, and opens your Crush's call over the lock screen. The tomorrow's-Crush reminder and the release alert work as on iPhone. All of this happens on your phone; nothing about your alarms is sent anywhere.

Alarm labels you type and your Crush's name can appear on your Lock Screen.

9. The age check

Snooze Crush is for ages 16 and up. On iPhone, where the law calls for it, the app asks your iPhone which age range you're in, using Apple's Declared Age Range feature. Apple decides what to share, based on your Apple Account and the choices you or your parent make, and the app learns only a range, never your birthday.

  • If the range shows you're under 16 (under 18 on the Brazil App Store), the app stops at a notice that it's for ages 16 and up (18 and up in Brazil), and doesn't connect to RevenueCat or our download server.
  • If it shows you're 16 or 17 anywhere else, the app offers only purchases with fixed contents, not draws.

The check runs on your iPhone. The age range is kept only in the app's memory while it's open. It's never saved, and never sent to us or anyone else.

On Android

On Android, the app asks Google Play for your age range each time it opens normally, using Google's Age Signals feature. Google decides whether to share it. It may first show its own question, and you (or your parent, for an account a parent manages with Family Link) can choose in the Play Store's settings whether Google shares it. Google shares only a range (under 13, 13 to 15, 16 or 17, or 18 and up), never your birthday, and may say how it knows (for example, that you told Google, or that a parent manages the account).

  • If the range shows you're under 16 (under 18 if your Google Play country is Brazil), the app stops at the 16+ notice (18+ in Brazil), and doesn't connect to RevenueCat or our download server. (Its check for app updates still runs, and sends no age or purchase information; see App updates.)
  • If it shows you're 16 or 17 anywhere else, or Google says your age still needs to be verified, the app offers only purchases with fixed contents, not draws.
  • If Google shares no range: in Brazil, the app offers only purchases with fixed contents, not draws. In the United States, the app asks you once for your birth month and year and uses your answer the same way (under 16, the 16+ notice; 16 or 17, purchases with fixed contents only; 18 and up, the whole app). If Google shares a range later, Google's range comes first. Elsewhere, you get the whole app.

If you give your birth month and year, the app saves them only on your phone, in a place Android's backup leaves out, so that it doesn't ask again. They're never sent to us or anyone else. The app has no way to change them; deleting the app or clearing its data removes them.

To tell where you are, the app reads your phone's region setting and your Google Play country, on your phone. The range becomes the app's decision at once and is then dropped. The app never saves or sends your age range, how Google knows it, or any other age information from Google; our servers never receive it, and it isn't in the alarm report.

11. Analytics and what Apple and Google tell us

The app has no analytics, advertising, or crash-reporting tools of its own. (The check for app updates sends an error's message only when an update stopped the app from starting, so that update can be undone; see App updates.) We do use RevenueCat's purchase dashboards for business analytics: they're built from the purchase records described in Purchases and RevenueCat and show us things like sales, refunds, and how many customers buy each product. That's why Apple's privacy label for Snooze Crush lists your purchase ID (“User ID”), your purchase history, the identifier iOS gives the app for your iPhone (“Device ID”) and the country RevenueCat works out from your IP address (“Coarse Location”) as used for “Analytics” as well as for the app's functions.

Apple also gives us App Store statistics, such as downloads and sales. If you've chosen in your iPhone's settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may also give us usage statistics and crash reports. Apple de-identifies these, and we use them only to find and fix problems. You can turn this sharing off in Settings › Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements.

On Android, Google Play's Data safety section for Snooze Crush lists your purchase ID (“User IDs”), your purchase history, and the country RevenueCat works out from your IP address (“Approximate location”), for the app's functions and analytics (the first two also for preventing fraud). It also lists the random install ID the app sends for app updates (“Device or other IDs”), for the app's functions and analytics, and the error message sent if an update stops the app from starting (“Crash logs”), for analytics (see App updates). An alarm report you share yourself goes only where you send it. Google gives us Google Play statistics, such as installs, ratings, and sales. If you've chosen to share usage and diagnostics with Google, Google may also give us crash reports and technical statistics without your name; we use them only to find and fix problems.

12. This website

phoenixdawnstudios.com is a simple, static website.

  • No cookies, no tracking. We don't set cookies or use tracking technologies such as tracking pixels or browser fingerprinting. The site follows your device's light or dark setting automatically and stores nothing in your browser. Its only script plays the Crushes' videos when you scroll to them.
  • No analytics or ads. The site has no analytics, advertising, or social-media tracking.
  • Server logs. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, run by GitHub, Inc. Like nearly every web host, GitHub automatically records standard technical information when you visit, including your IP address, for security, under the GitHub General Privacy Statement. We don't receive these logs.
