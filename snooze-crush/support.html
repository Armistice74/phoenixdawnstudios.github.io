Frequently asked questions

My alarm didn't ring on my iPhone. What should I check?

Sorry about that. Nobody likes a missed morning. Most missed alarms come down to a phone setting. Here's what to check:

  • Ring in Silent mode: on iOS 26.2 and later, open Snooze Crush's Settings and check Ring in Silent mode. When it's on, your alarms ring like the Clock app's, even in Silent mode and Focus.
  • Permissions: go to your iPhone's Settings › Apps › Snooze Crush and make sure Notifications, and Alarms where it's listed, are allowed.
  • If alarms arrive as notifications (on iOS 26.0 and 26.1, or if you haven't allowed alarms): they don't make a sound in Silent mode, so keep Silent mode off overnight. To let them through a Focus like Sleep, go to Settings › Focus, choose your Focus, and allow Snooze Crush or its Time Sensitive notifications.
  • Stay up to date: keep both the app and iOS updated. We fix bugs and adapt to system changes as they come out.

Still stuck? Use Report a problem in the app's Settings, or email us with your iPhone model, iOS version, and the time the alarm was set for.

My alarm didn't ring on my Android phone. What should I check?

Sorry about that. On Android, a few settings decide whether an alarm can ring. If one is off, Snooze Crush usually shows a banner on Home or Alarms with a Fix button that opens the right page. Menu names differ a little between phone makers.

  • Notifications: Settings › Apps › Snooze Crush › Notifications. Keep them on, and keep Ringing alarms on and not silenced.
  • Full-screen notifications (Android 14 and later): Settings › Apps › Special app access › Full screen notifications › Snooze Crush. This lets your Crush's call open over the lock screen.
  • Alarms & reminders (Android 12): Settings › Apps › Special app access › Alarms & reminders › Snooze Crush. On Android 13 and later it's always allowed.
  • Battery: Settings › Apps › Snooze Crush › App battery usage. Don't choose Restricted. Some phone makers add their own battery savers: if your phone has a list of “sleeping” apps, keep Snooze Crush off it, and if it has an “auto-start” setting, allow Snooze Crush.
  • Don't force-stop the app. “Force stop” in Settings cancels your alarms until you open Snooze Crush again. Swiping it away from your recent apps is fine.
  • Do Not Disturb: alarms ring through Do Not Disturb as long as it allows alarms (in Settings, under Do Not Disturb, check that Alarms are allowed).
  • After a restart, your alarms are booked again on their own. If you haven't unlocked your phone since the restart, the alarm still rings, and your Crush's call opens after you unlock it.
  • Stay up to date: keep both the app and Android updated.

Still stuck? Go to Settings › Help › Share alarm report, choose your email app, and send the report to hello@phoenixdawnstudios.com with the time the alarm was set for. It shows us what your phone's alarm system did that night (see the next question). Or email us with your phone's maker and model, Android version, and the time the alarm was set for.

What's the “alarm report” on Android, and how do I send it?

It's a plain-text record that helps us see why an alarm or a purchase didn't work. To send it:

  1. Open Snooze Crush and go to Settings › Help.
  2. Tap Share alarm report. Your phone's share sheet opens.
  3. Choose your email app (Gmail, for example), send it to hello@phoenixdawnstudios.com, and tell us what went wrong and when.

The app sends nothing by itself: the report goes only where you choose, and you can read it before you send it. It holds the app's version, your Android version and phone model, your alarms' own log (times, which Crush rang, sounds and volume steps), and the steps of the app's purchase checks since it last started. It never holds your name, email, purchase ID, order numbers, or your age or birth date. Our Privacy Policy lists everything in it.

Tip: if a purchase or Restore just failed, send the report right away, before you close the app. The purchase steps are kept only until the app is fully closed.

Why does Snooze Crush ask to “display over other apps” on Android?

It's optional. While you're using your phone, Android normally shows a ringing alarm as a banner at the top, and you tap it to answer. With Display over other apps turned on, your Crush's call opens right away, the way it does on iPhone. Snooze Crush uses this only to open the call when an alarm rings, and never draws anything over other apps. Your alarms ring either way. You can change it in the app's Settings › Alarms, or in your phone's Settings › Apps › Special app access › Display over other apps.

The alarm is too quiet (or too loud). How do I fix the volume?
  • The alarm sound: like Clock alarms, Snooze Crush's alarms follow your Ringtone and Alerts volume in Settings › Sounds & Haptics. (The side buttons change it only if Change with Buttons is on.)
  • Your Crush's call: the voice and video play at media volume. Turn it up with the side buttons while the call is on screen.
  • Headphones and speakers: if you're connected to Bluetooth, sound may play through that device. Disconnect before bed if you want the alarm to come from your phone.
  • On Android: alarms follow your phone's Alarm volume (press a volume button, then the settings or three-dot icon on the volume slider). If it's very low, Snooze Crush raises it to an audible level while the alarm rings and sets it back afterward, and plays your Crush's call at the same loudness as the alarm.
How do Packs and draws work?

Packs are on the Packs tab. Each Pack has 5 Crushes, plus a Secret Crush you get with the complete set.

  • A draw gives you one Crush you don't have yet. You never get a repeat, so 5 draws always complete a Pack. The odds show right above the Draw button before you buy.
  • The whole pack, or “the other N” after one to three draws, gives you every Crush you're missing, plus the Secret Crush. No surprises.
  • In some countries (Japan, for example), the App Store and Google Play sell only the whole pack.

When you buy Crushes, you get a license to use them in Snooze Crush on your Apple Account or Google Account, not ownership. They have no cash value and can't be traded, gifted, or shared through Family Sharing or Google Play Family Library. Our Terms of Use have the details.

Why did a draw give me the same Crush I had before?

The order your draws give Crushes in is shuffled once for your Apple Account and never changes. That's what lets Restore Purchases give you back exactly the same Crushes on any iPhone.

It also means that if a draw was refunded and you draw again, you get the same Crush that refunded draw gave you. There's no way to re-roll. When that's the case, the app names that Crush on the Pack's page, shows its odds as 100%, and asks you to confirm before you buy.

On Android, it works the same way for your Google Account. The one exception: if every Snooze Crush purchase on that Google Account was refunded, a phone that has never had the app (or one whose app data was cleared) can't find your old order and starts a new one, with honest odds. Our Terms of Use explain it.

How do I restore my purchases on a new phone?

Usually you don't have to do anything: the app checks your purchases when it opens. If your Crushes don't show up, open the Packs tab and tap Restore Purchases (it's also in Settings › Help). Make sure you're signed in to the same Apple Account you used to buy them, in Settings › your name › Media & Purchases. There's no separate account with us to create or remember.

On Android: it works the same way with your Google Account. Your Crushes belong to the Google Account that bought them, the one selected in the Play Store. To check, open the Play Store, tap your profile picture, and choose that account. Then open Snooze Crush: your Crushes come back on their own, or tap Restore Purchases (on the Packs tab, or in Settings › Help). Restore needs an internet connection, because the app checks with Google Play and our servers to find your Crushes.

  • “No pack purchases were found for this Google Account”: the Play Store is probably set to a different account. Switch to the one you bought with, then try again.
  • “Couldn't restore right now” with a short code such as (R-10): nothing is lost. Check your connection and try again a little later. The code tells us what went wrong (see the next question).
On Android, a message ends in a short code like (P-10) or (R-20). What does it mean?

It's a support code. It holds no personal information, so it's safe to send us. The letter says where the problem happened, and the part after the dash says what went wrong:

  • P: loading the Packs page (“Purchases aren't available right now”). R: Restore Purchases (“Couldn't restore right now”).
  • 10 or 35: the app couldn't connect. Check your Wi-Fi or mobile data and try again.
  • 2: Google Play had a problem. Make sure the Play Store app is up to date and signed in, then try again later.
  • 15: a check was already running. Wait a moment, then try again.
  • Any other number, or a letter (a letter means the step where it stopped): try again later. If it keeps happening, email us the code, or send us an alarm report (Settings › Help › Share alarm report) right after it happens.
Can I get a refund?

On iPhone, purchases are made through the App Store, so Apple handles refunds under its policies. Request one at reportaproblem.apple.com. We can't issue App Store refunds ourselves, and we never see your payment details.

On Android, purchases are made through Google Play, under Google Play's refund policies. You can ask Google: on play.google.com, choose your profile picture › Payments & subscriptions › Budget & order history, then Report a problem next to the order. More than 48 hours after buying, Google suggests asking us: email us with your Google Play order number (it starts with “GPA.”, and it's in Google's receipt email). We look at every request and can refund through Google Play. We never see your payment details.

If Apple or Google refunds a purchase, the app removes what it gave you: the Crush from a refunded draw (or the Crushes from a refunded whole pack or “other N”), and the Pack's Secret Crush if you no longer have all 5. Days you'd planned with a removed Crush go back to May Day.

Good to know before you ask for a refund on a draw: a refund doesn't re-roll it. Your draw order is fixed for your Apple Account or Google Account, so if you draw again in that Pack, you get the same Crush back. The app tells you which Crush it is before you buy.

If something went wrong with a purchase, like a Crush that didn't show up, let us know too. We're happy to help sort it out.

How do I delete my data?

Your alarms, Crush plans, and settings are stored only on your phone. To delete them, delete the app. On iPhone, “Offload App” keeps your data, so choose “Delete App”. On Android, uninstall the app (archiving keeps your data).

Our purchase service, RevenueCat, keeps a record of your purchases under a code made from your Apple Account. To have it deleted, email hello@phoenixdawnstudios.com with the Order ID from one of your Snooze Crush receipts. Deleting it doesn't remove your purchases from your Apple Account, and the app creates the record again the next time it opens on an iPhone signed in to that account. On Android, send the Google Play order number instead (it starts with “GPA.”; a purchase redeemed with a promo code has none); the same applies, and we keep the small address-book line that lets your Crushes come back on a new phone. Our Privacy Policy explains this in full.

Which devices are supported?

Snooze Crush is made for iPhone and needs iOS 26.0 or later. Alarms that ring through Silent mode and Focus need iOS 26.2 or later. Buying, restoring, and downloading Crushes need an internet connection; alarms don't. There's no Android version.

Who is the app for?

Snooze Crush is for ages 16 and up (18 and up in Brazil). If you're under 18, a parent or guardian needs to agree to our Terms of Use. The Crushes are playful and a little flirty, but never explicit, and all of them are fictional adults.

Do I need an account?

Nope. There's no sign-up, no login, and no password to forget. Purchases go through your Apple Account on iPhone, or your Google Account in the Play Store on Android.

On Android, the app says my purchase was made on another Google Account. What happened?

Your phone has more than one Google Account, and the purchase was paid for by a different account from the one whose Crushes the app is showing. The Crushes belong to the account that paid. To see them, choose that account in the Play Store (tap your profile picture), then open Snooze Crush again. If it still looks wrong, email us with the Google Play order number (it starts with “GPA.”).

Can I use the app in Japanese?

Yes. Choose English or 日本語 in the app's Settings › Language, or let it follow your phone's language. In Japanese, the Crushes' lines are dubbed over the same animation, so the lips won't always match perfectly.

How do I report a bug or a line that sounds wrong?

Thank you for helping us improve! In the app, go to Settings › Help and tap Report a problem or Suggest a fix to a line. Both open an email with the details we need already filled in. You can also flag a line during a call and send the report from the screen at the end of the call. If you'd rather write yourself, email us and include:

  • your phone model and its iOS or Android version;
  • the app version (on iPhone: Settings › General › iPhone Storage › Snooze Crush; on Android: Settings › Apps › Snooze Crush, at the bottom of the page);
  • what you expected to happen, and what happened instead;
  • steps to make it happen again, if you can;
  • for alarm issues: the time the alarm was set for and which day (on Android, also attach an alarm report from Settings › Help › Share alarm report); and
  • a screenshot or screen recording, if it helps show the problem.
